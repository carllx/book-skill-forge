# Workflow: Analyzing Sculptural Narrative & Temporality (Krauss, Community 1)

## 1. Prerequisites & Context
**WHEN TO USE:**
Execute this workflow when evaluating a modern sculpture's approach to temporality, narrative sequence, or its phenomenological impact on the viewer. It is particularly useful for analyzing the transition from classical, structurally legible statuary to modern, surface-oriented objects.

**WHY IT MATTERS:**
Modern sculpture shifts away from traditional "classical" legibility—where internal anatomical logic dictates outward form—toward a modern "opaque" or "synchronous" temporality. Here, meaning does not precede experience but coalesces *within* the act of perception on the surface itself.

## 2. Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### Heuristic 1: Assess Contour Clarity vs. Surface Fusion
Evaluate whether the sculptural form allows the viewer to "read back" from the surface configuration to an underlying skeletal or structural core.
* **Classical Approach:** Bodies are deployed with explicit frontality and clear contours. The mechanics of stress and weight are logically resolved (e.g., Canova's *Hercules and Lichas* forms a single wedgelike shape defining structural stress).
* **Modern Approach (Rodin):** Contours are fused and bodies are seamed together, creating ambiguity. 
  > **Show, Don't Tell:** In Rodin's *Je suis belle*, the bodies of the male and female figures are fused into a single contour. The gesture reads simultaneously as collapse and expansion, creating an opacity that your anatomical knowledge cannot rationally penetrate.

### Heuristic 2: Identify the Temporal Condition of the Surface
Investigate if the surface acts as an "event" itself, documenting the temporal *process* of making rather than purely describing an underlying anatomy.
* **Guideline:** Look for retained casting flaws, untempered modeling marks, or deep gouges that force the viewer to acknowledge the work as the result of an act over time.
  > **Show, Don't Tell:** Rodin’s *Man's Torso* (1877) retains air-pocket holes and casting ridges, while his *Flying Figure* shows an unpatched knife cut on the calf muscle. The surface meaning occurs where the externalization of gesture meets the imprint of the artist's manipulation.
* **Surface Coalescence (Rosso & Art Nouveau):** Apply this to surfaces that shroud the underlying object to capture a fleeting moment or external force (e.g., wind or water erosion in Guimard's furniture, or the momentary fusion of timidity and curiosity in Medardo Rosso's *Ecce Puer!*).

### Heuristic 3: Contrast "Synthetic Vision" with "Immanent Logic"
Determine if the object attempts to transcend physical limitations via an ideal form, or if it fully embraces literal space by combining physical textures and descriptive language.
* **Futurist Synthetic Vision:** Attempts to give the stationary viewer a conceptual omnipotence over the object, collapsing space and time.
  > **Show, Don't Tell:** Boccioni's *Development of a Bottle in Space* presents nested, revolving half-cylinders around an ideal, static concavity. The stationary viewer is conceptually freed to grasp the object from all sides simultaneously.
* **Cubist Immanent Logic:** Disperses the object's contour into tactile cues and decorative pieces on a literal surface.
  > **Show, Don't Tell:** In Picasso's *Violin* (1914) or *Guitar*, traditional illusionistic devices (like crosshatching or converging perspective lines) are rendered physically. Because they exist in 3D space, these elements become redundant for describing volume and turn into "ossified" decorative fragments of a descriptive language.

## 3. If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

* **IF** a figure's gesture appears anatomically awkward, unbalanced, or distorted (e.g., Rodin's *Adam*), 
  **THEN** do not dismiss it as a failure of anatomical representation. **Action:** Analyze the opacity as an intentional assertion. The figure's torment is an effect functioning without a logical anatomical cause, demanding that meaning be experienced synchronously with the viewing.

* **IF** you encounter identical, repetitive sculptural units within a single work (e.g., the threefold appearance of *The Shades* on the *Gates of Hell*), 
  **THEN** shift your analysis away from linear narrative progression. **Action:** Evaluate how the repetition halts the narrative, forcing a self-conscious focus on the process of creation and replication itself.

* **IF** traditional 2D illusionistic elements (perspective lines, shading marks) are found on a 3D constructed object, 
  **THEN** recognize them as displaced descriptive cues. **Action:** Interpret these as frozen linguistic fragments that Picasso forces to exist in literal, rather than virtual, space.
