# Workflow: Forms of Readymade (Duchamp and Brancusi)

## Prerequisites & Context
**WHY & WHEN to use this workflow:**
Trigger this workflow when analyzing modern sculptures or conceptual pieces that utilize found objects, mechanistic logic, or extreme depersonalization (e.g., Duchamp's readymades, Brancusi's hyper-polished geometries). This framework provides heuristics for decoding art that deliberately short-circuits traditional "autographic" meaning, substituting emotional expression with strategic, impersonal inquiry.

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. The Strategy of Depersonalization
When evaluating readymades or hyper-polished forms, recognize their intentional severance from the artist's psychological "signature." 
- **Identify the Mechanized Process**: Look for rules, homophonic puns, or arbitrary systems that generate the work without the artist's emotional intervention. 
- **Subvert Formal Analysis**: Do not attempt to decode formal relationships (part-to-part composition) in works like the *Fountain* or Brancusi's *Bird in Space*. They are designed to be formally irreducible.

> *Example (Mechanized Authorship)*: "But by automating the production of art, the machines arrive at a result in which the structure of the image is absolutely disconnected from the psychological and emotional structure of the person who initiates the art..."

### 2. Contextual Transposition as the "Work"
Recognize that in these forms, the act of physical creation is replaced by the act of selection, inversion, and placement.
- **Analyze the Inversion**: Note how a 90-degree rotation (as in *Fountain*) transforms an ordinary industrial object into a theoretical question.
- **Placement Dictates Meaning**: For unitary objects (like Brancusi's *Beginning of the World*), evaluate how placement on a specific pedestal or reflective disk alters its geometry through environmental reflection rather than internal sculpting.

> *Example (Placement & Geometry)*: "Polished to mirrorlike smoothness, the bronze 'egg' is placed in the center of a circular metal disk... Where the object touches base with the disk, the reflection it receives is the shadow of its own nether side... placement is all."

### 3. The Fusion of the Mechanical and the Erotic
Detect the recurring synthesis of industrial, machine-like forms with veiled or overt eroticism, a common throughline for both Duchamp and Brancusi.
- **Trace the Anthropomorphic**: Observe how sleek, manufactured items are forced to read as human or sexual forms (e.g., *Fountain* as a uterine form, Brancusi's *Torso of a Young Man* reading as a sublimated phallus).
- **Evaluate the Subtext**: Use the erotic layer not as a definitive metaphor, but as a mechanism that creates tension with the object's cold, impersonal origin.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

| Condition / Observation | Action / Heuristic |
| :--- | :--- |
| **IF** you attempt a standard formal analysis (e.g., mass vs. void) and find no internal relationships (the object is a pure ovoid or unmodified bottle rack)... | **THEN** pivot from *structural* analysis to *strategic* analysis. Ask: What act of transposition or selection occurred? Defer to `graphify_query` for the object's exhibition history. |
| **IF** the narrative seems cyclical, turning the viewer back to the question of "Why is this art?" without resolution... | **THEN** identify this as the "circular shape of a quandary" characteristic of the readymade. The object is a strategy to question the aesthetic context, not an end-product. |
| **IF** the work relies on a physically transparent ground (e.g., Duchamp's *Glider*)... | **THEN** recognize the rejection of the illusionistic matrix. The transparent ground exposes the object to the real space of the viewer, destroying the "suspension of disbelief." |
| **IF** you encounter Brancusi using industrial materials (polished bronze) in defiance of natural wood or stone properties... | **THEN** reject the "truth-to-materials" hypothesis. Frame the finish as an active pursuit of machine-made sleekness and an "effacement of self." |
