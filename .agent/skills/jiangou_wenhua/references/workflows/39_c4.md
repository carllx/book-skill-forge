# Workflow: The Tectonic Trajectory (1903-1994)

## Prerequisites & Context
**WHY & WHEN to use this workflow:**
This workflow distills the architectural tectonic history from 1903 to 1994 into actionable design heuristics. It should be applied when evaluating or designing structural forms, material expressions, and the interplay between a building and its topography. It focuses on the structural poetics developed by masters like Berlage, Wagner, Le Corbusier, Hertzberger, and Fehn.

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Express Structure as Generative Form
Do not treat structure as a hidden necessity; allow it to generate the architectural language and spatial experience.
- **Material Authenticity:** Use materials in ways that reflect their structural properties (e.g., masonry in compression, steel in tension).
- **Tectonic Legibility:** Articulate the difference between load-bearing elements (the skeleton) and non-load-bearing elements (the skin/infill).
> "优秀的建筑总是始于有效的建造（construction），没有建造便没有建筑... 关键在于，我们始终要发扬建造的精神，因地制宜，而不是将格格不入的思想强加于建造的地点。" (Good architecture always starts with effective construction... The key is to promote the spirit of construction, adapting to local conditions rather than imposing incompatible ideas.)

### 2. Transmute and Contrast Materials (The Skin)
The building's skin is an opportunity to communicate tectonic tension and material poetry.
- **Material Metallization/Petrification:** Experiment with making heavy materials appear light, or light materials appear heavy. For example, Otto Wagner used iron bolts to affix marble panels, giving stone the woven texture of a textile or armor.
- **Exoskeletal Framing:** Consider moving the structural framework outside the envelope to free the interior and emphasize the structural forces holding the building together.

**Reference:**
![Otto Wagner, Vienna Post Office Savings Bank](../../images/a8d69c87c38ac5d5967fa1ceeaa5d204355575e01fda0131fcd2ef5eb43ed39b.jpg)

### 3. Anchor the Earthwork and Elevate the Roofwork
A building must resolve its relationship with the ground and the sky.
- **Going-to-Ground:** Treat the base of the building as an extension of the topography. Use retaining walls, semi-subterranean spaces, or heavy rustication to evoke geological permanence.
- **The Roof as Landscape:** The roof can act as an expressive canopy or suspended tent that defines the environmental and spatial boundaries (e.g., Sverre Fehn's Nordic Pavilion integrating existing trees into the roof plane).

**Reference:**
![Sverre Fehn, Nordic Pavilion](../../images/dc6f2c5f2a04b63c48bd291c7b34f8677798010ed1a9289e91d3380889d0c332.jpg)

### 4. Micro-Tectonics and Social Interaction
Architectural elements should facilitate human scale and social cohesion.
- **Spatial Generative Structures:** Use the rhythm of columns, beams, or drop floors to delineate "rooms" without building full enclosure walls.
- **Transitional Thresholds (Saku):** Design highly articulated micro-spaces—such as sills, sunken pits, and benches—that encourage gathering and provide a sense of protection.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

- **IF** the structural system feels arbitrary or purely utilitarian, **THEN** identify opportunities to express the structural joints or connections visually, or use micro-tectonic details (railings, sills) to scale the structure down to human perception.
- **IF** the building feels disconnected from its site, **THEN** re-evaluate the "earthwork." Modify the foundation to integrate with the terrain's contours or use local masonry to establish a geological root.
- **IF** the interior spaces lack hierarchy or social cohesion, **THEN** introduce "hollows" or "pits" (like Hertzberger's sunken seating) or use structural bays to subconsciously organize distinct social zones.
