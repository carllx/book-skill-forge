# Auguste Perret & Classical Rationalism: Workflow & Heuristics

## Prerequisites & Context

**WHEN to use this workflow:**
This workflow applies when you are designing, analyzing, or evaluating architectural systems that utilize exposed structural frames (especially reinforced concrete) and seek to marry classical tectonic rationality with modern structural logic. It is particularly relevant when navigating the transition from load-bearing masonry to framed structures while retaining a culturally resonant, monumental, or civic character.

**WHY this matters:**
Auguste Perret’s approach (Structural Classicism) represents a profound attempt to reconcile the "Greek-Gothic ideal" with the monolithic nature of reinforced concrete. Rather than hiding the frame or employing pastiche, Perret treated the structural framework as the primary poetic and expressive element of architecture, establishing a systematic, rule-based approach to proportion, scale, and material truth.

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Frame vs. Infill: The Tectonic Hierarchy
Treat the structural frame and the non-load-bearing infill as distinct architectural elements. Do not blur their roles.
*   **Actionable Guideline:** Always articulate the difference between the primary supporting framework and the secondary enclosing walls. 
*   **Heuristic:** Use material differentiation to highlight this contrast. For utilitarian spaces, leave the concrete frame bare and use brick infill. For civic faces, you may use stone or plaster but maintain the legibility of the frame.
> **Example (Rue Franklin 25):** Perret applied ceramic tiles over the concrete frame to differentiate it from the infill, rendering the framework legible as a distinct system.

### 2. The Truth of Construction (Vraisemblance)
The form should reflect the construction method, specifically the formwork (the wooden molds used to cast the concrete).
*   **Actionable Guideline:** Embrace the marks of construction. If utilizing concrete, recognize that wooden formwork is its prerequisite. 
*   **Heuristic:** Translate the logic of wooden joinery (the *charpente*) into the concrete detailing. Do not simply imitate stone masonry. 
> **Example (Notre-Dame du Raincy):** The columns feature fluting that echoes both Doric columns and Gothic bundle-pillars, a texture directly derived from the wooden formwork.

### 3. Progressive Structural Expression (Hierarchy of Facades)
Adapt the structural expression according to the socio-cultural or utilitarian status of the building's facade.
*   **Actionable Guideline:** Use bare, exposed concrete (beton brut) for highly utilitarian areas (e.g., back facades, warehouses). For primary or monumental facades, you may dress the frame, but never obscure its structural rhythm.
*   **Heuristic:** Create a clear gradation of finishes. If the volume shifts to a utilitarian "backstage," immediately transition to expressing the bare fireproofed concrete frame.

### 4. Proportion and Rhythm (The Paratactic Configuration)
Establish a rhythmic, classical order (like the ABABA Palladian rhythm) across the building's grid.
*   **Actionable Guideline:** Vary bay sizes (e.g., small-large-small-large-small) to create spatial symphonies and structural punctuation.
*   **Heuristic:** Use paired columns or pilasters to emphasize larger spans and corners, bringing a definitive "end" to the colonnade.

### 5. The "Fenetre en Hauteur" (The French Window)
Use the vertical French window as the primary aperture rather than the horizontal strip window.
*   **Actionable Guideline:** Frame the human figure and the complete space (street, garden, sky) using vertical windows.
*   **Heuristic:** Ensure that windows can be closed off, defining a discrete "territory" for the room, contrary to the endless, uncontained panorama of the strip window.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

*   **IF** the structural joints (e.g., beam-to-column) appear overly heavy or lack expressive power, **THEN** avoid simple haunched beams (corbels) that suppress architectural expression at the point of maximum stress. Instead, uniform the cross-sections at the intersection to clarify the frame's logic.
*   **IF** the concrete surface appears monolithic and lacks human scale, **THEN** introduce bush-hammering (bouchardage) to expose the aggregate, contrasting it with smooth precast elements, or utilize standard mill section formwork to impart a modular, tectonic texture to the surface.
*   **IF** you are designing a highly utilitarian structure (e.g., a garage or warehouse), **THEN** do not mask the structure with classical cladding. Expose the raw framework immediately after the formwork is removed.
*   **IF** the building feels like a mere container rather than an articulated civic presence, **THEN** introduce a secondary order, such as a freestanding peripheral colonnade outside the enclosing wall, to establish depth and monumental scale (as seen in the Musee des Travaux Publics).

## Verification Checklists

### Structural Legibility
- [ ] Is the primary structural frame visibly distinct from the infill material?
- [ ] Are the joints and connections articulated to reflect the flow of loads?
- [ ] Does the facade clearly communicate which elements are bearing weight and which are merely enclosures?

### Material & Tectonic Truth
- [ ] Does the concrete (if used) express its origin in wooden formwork rather than imitating carved stone?
- [ ] Are utilitarian/rear facades treated with appropriately raw structural expression compared to civic fronts?
- [ ] Have surface treatments (e.g., bush-hammering, exposed aggregate) been used strategically to differentiate precast vs. in-situ elements?

### Spatial & Proportion Logic
- [ ] Is there a discernible rhythmic pattern (e.g., alternating bay sizes) organizing the plan and elevation?
- [ ] Do the apertures (windows) frame human activity and create distinct interior territories?
- [ ] Are corners and terminations of the building resolved definitively (e.g., through thickened or paired columns)?
