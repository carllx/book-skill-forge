# Workflow: Tracking Stylistic Evolution and Degeneration (Mannerism)

## Objective
To map the trajectory of an artistic movement (like Florentine Mannerism) from its intense, innovative beginnings to its polished but hollow decline, encompassing both painting and sculpture.

## Heuristics & Principles
1. **The Spectrum of Innovation:** Identify the diverse early manifestations of a style (e.g., Pontormo's emotional intensity vs. Bronzino's cold, chic eroticism).
2. **The Danger of Formula:** Recognize how a highly stylized movement can degenerate into "elegant but empty posturing" when subsequent artists (like Vasari) prioritize aesthetic formulas over underlying substance.
3. **Cross-Medium Flourishing:** An established style often jumps from 2D to 3D. Look for how sculptural forms (Cellini, Giambologna) adopt the elongated, twisting aesthetics of the movement.

## Steps for Analysis
1. **Define the Geographic Center:** Identify the primary city or court driving the movement (e.g., Florence).
2. **Analyze the Pioneers:** Examine the distinct emotional or psychological approaches of the movement's early masters.
3. **Identify the Point of Degeneration:** Look for later works where technical elegance and artificiality have completely overshadowed emotional or narrative purpose.
4. **Examine the Sculptural Counterparts:** Analyze how the movement's defining traits (e.g., the *figura serpentinata*) are realized in three dimensions by its leading sculptors.
