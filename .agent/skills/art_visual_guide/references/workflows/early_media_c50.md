# Workflow: Early Media, Tempera & Oil Painting Mastery

## Prerequisites & Context

**When to use this workflow:**
This workflow applies when analyzing, replicating, or understanding the technical progression and material conditions of medieval, Renaissance, and early modern painting media. It focuses on the shift from tempera and fresco to oil painting, exploring support preparation, pigment characteristics, and layer building techniques.

**Why it matters:**
Understanding these historical techniques is critical for tracing regional origins (e.g., wood panel types), recognizing artist constraints (e.g., fast-drying tempera vs. slow-drying oils), and identifying preservation artifacts (e.g., darkening varnishes or giomate joins in frescoes).

> **Deep Dive Reference:** For bulk theory regarding the specific chemical compositions of binders, exhaustive geographical variations of wood panels, or historical context surrounding individual masters like Duccio or Giotto, defer to the `graphify_query` MCP tool with the topic `Early media` or `Egg tempera`.

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Support Selection and Preparation

**Identifying the Support Formats:**
- **Regional Wood Indicators (Pre-16th Century):**
  - *Italy:* Poplar.
  - *Flanders:* Oak.
  - *Germany:* Pine, fir, lime, or oak.
- **Canvas Transition:** Emerging strongly in Venice to provide a lighter, more flexible support.

**Preparation Heuristics:**
- **For Panel:**
  1. Plane glued wood planks until smooth.
  2. Apply several layers of white gesso or chalk mixed with animal skin glue.
  3. Seal with an isolation layer to prevent paint sinking.
- **For Canvas:**
  1. Stretch linen taut.
  2. Coat with a glue-sizing ground.

### 2. Executing Egg Tempera

**Characteristics:**
- **Binder:** Egg yolk mixed with water to a creamy paste.
- **Behavior:** Dries in minutes; highly stable, vibrant, and durable.

**Execution Steps:**
1. **Underdrawing:** Create a detailed sketch directly on the thinnest, topmost layer of gesso. (This will often show through thin paint layers.)
2. **Layering:** Because it dries instantly, you *cannot* blend or use impasto. Build form using small, meticulous hatching strokes side-by-side.
3. **Gilding Integration:** Apply mordant size to glue thin gold leaf directly onto the panel, following historical (e.g., Byzantine) traditions for specific elements like robes or halos.

### 3. Transitioning to Oils and Glazing

**Characteristics:**
- **Binder:** Linseed oil (standard), walnut oil (16th C. Italy), or poppy seed oil (Dutch/French). Olive oil does not dry properly.
- **Behavior:** Dries slowly by oxidation. Allows for leisurely, seamless blending without visible brushmarks.

**Execution Steps (Glazing - Van Eyck Method):**
1. **Base Layer:** Optionally start with an opaque underlayer of tempera.
2. **Glazing:** Apply thin, transparent layers of oil paint. Let each layer dry fully before the next.
3. **Optical Mixing:** Layering acts like "sheets of cellophane", modifying colors above and below to create deep, luminous effects and mimic exact light fall.
4. **Finishing:** Varnish the completed work to intensify color, reveal detail, and add gloss. (Note: Anticipate varnish darkening over time).

> "All [oil painting] asks of the artist is care and love, for oil in itself possesses the property of making colour smoother, softer, more delicate..." — *Giorgio Vasari, 1550*

### 4. Pigment Preparation and Selection

- **Grinding:** Grind raw earth or rare pigments continuously until fine enough to evenly suspend in the binder.
- **Azurite:** A cheaper alternative to ultramarine. *Heuristic:* Expect a green tinge when ground fine; unsuitable for pure skies.
- **Lead White:** Highly reflective, creates stark contrast. *Actionable tip:* In oil painting, use lead white as an active drying agent to speed up the process.
- **Blacks:** Differentiate between carbon-based charcoal (cooler) and bone black (warmer, precursor to ivory black).

### 5. Fresco Execution and Planning

- **The *Giomate* System:** You must estimate exactly how much wall area can be plastered and painted in a single day before the plaster dries.
- **Complexity Planning:** Allocate full days for complex areas (e.g., faces) and larger sections for flat colors.
- **Color Matching:** Meticulously match colors at the "joins" (giomate) between days to maintain continuity.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

| Condition / Observation | Likely Cause / Implication | Action / Resolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **If** you observe small, distinct hatching strokes instead of smooth blends... | **Then** the artist likely used egg tempera due to its rapid drying time. | Verify by checking for a rigid support (wood panel) and lack of thick impasto. |
| **If** the panel support is made of Poplar... | **Then** the origin is almost certainly Italian (14th-16th C). | Cross-reference with stylistic elements like extensive gilding or Byzantine influences. |
| **If** visible "joins" or slight color mismatches occur in a mural... | **Then** you are observing *giomate* boundaries in a fresco. | Map the joins to understand the artist's daily work rate and section planning. |
| **If** a blue pigment shows a distinct greenish tinge... | **Then** it is likely Azurite rather than expensive Ultramarine. | Note the economic choices of the workshop; use for undercoats or specific non-sky elements. |
| **If** an oil painting's surface appears universally darkened or yellowed... | **Then** the protective varnish has aged. | Acknowledge that the colors beneath may still be vibrant; reference conservation methods for varnish removal. |

## Verification Checklists

**Material & Support Audit**
- [ ] Support material identified (Wood species or Canvas).
- [ ] Gesso/Chalk ground layers verified for smoothness.
- [ ] Isolation/sizing layer applied (prevents paint sinking).

**Medium & Application Audit**
- [ ] **Tempera:** Applied via hatching? Fast drying properties accommodated?
- [ ] **Oil:** Seamless blending achieved? Glazes applied sequentially after drying?
- [ ] **Fresco:** *Giomate* correctly sized for a single day's work?

**Pigment & Final Finish Audit**
- [ ] Pigments properly ground for suspension.
- [ ] Lead white utilized for highlights and as a drying agent in oils.
- [ ] Final varnish applied (for oils) to increase gloss and saturation.
