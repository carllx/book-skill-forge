# Fresco & Classical Mural Techniques: Actionable Workflow

## Prerequisites & Context

**Why and When to Use This Workflow:**
This workflow serves as a heuristic guide for understanding, analyzing, or simulating traditional fresco and large-scale mural painting methodologies. It translates the deep historical context of Italian frescoes, Cubist-influenced expressive art, and monumental ceiling paintings into actionable steps for artists, restorers, or digital painters.

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Preparation & Material Dynamics
Fresco requires absolute synchronicity between the artist's preparation and the drying time of the plaster.

*   **The "Cartoon" Method:** Always trace a detailed drawn plan (the cartoon) onto the wall before applying paint. Do not attempt direct painting without a structured plan.
*   **True Fresco ("Buon Fresco") Execution:** Paint water-based pigments straight onto wet plaster. The wall MUST be painted in sections that can be completed before drying.
    *   *Heuristic:* A single head of a key figure typically takes an entire day. Plaster only the specific area of the wall you intend to finish that day.
*   **Climate Consideration:** This technique is primarily practical in dry climates; damp environments will fundamentally damage the plaster before curing.

> "Fresco is only practical in dry climates like that of Italy, as damp damages plaster."

### 2. Composition & Breaking Convention
Leverage historical methods for organizing complex scenes and challenging established norms.

*   **Individualization over Generics:** Create numerous studies to give unique features and psychological depth ("intention of man's soul") to individual figures.
    *   *Example:* Leonardo's meticulous recreation of elderly features (grey hair, sunken eye sockets) for Apostle Jude, as seen in his studies.
*   **Spatial and Perspective Mastery:** When dealing with ceiling art or domed surfaces, use foreshortening to create an illusion of enormous space, breaking the physical confines of the room (e.g., Andrea Pozzo's *Glory of St Ignatius Loyola* or Johann Michael Rottmayr's flattened domes).
*   **Stylistic Fusion:** Do not fear combining disparate influences. Merge expressive emotionalism with angular, geometric forms (e.g., Cubist influences) to create rhythm and unity in sculptural or flat works.

### 3. Restoration Principles
When dealing with damaged works (e.g., earthquake damage), adhere strictly to preservation ethics.

*   **No Falsification:** Try to replace any missing fragments. NEVER hide the original work or attempt to "improve" it with additional, modern paint.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

| Condition | Action / Heuristic |
| :--- | :--- |
| **If** the plaster is drying faster than the painting process can keep up... | **Then** immediately stop painting that section. Painting on dry walls ("fresco secco") means the paint will eventually flake off as it hasn't bonded. Chisel away and re-plaster a smaller, manageable section. |
| **If** the composition feels emotionally flat or generic... | **Then** refer back to individual character studies. Ensure that each figure's gestures and expressions reflect a distinct internal state, breaking away from "row-like" static compositions. |
| **If** the physical space (like a ceiling) feels restrictive... | **Then** deploy dramatic foreshortening and architectural perspective illusions (trompe l'oeil) to digitally or physically "open" the space upward. |
| **If** restoring a damaged historical piece... | **Then** focus solely on stabilizing and fragment replacement. Do not add speculative "improvements" that overlay the original hand of the artist. |

> [!WARNING]
> Unwillingness to submit to the restriction of plastering only what can be painted in a day can have disastrous consequences, as experienced by Leonardo da Vinci.

## Verification Checklists

### Execution Checklist
- [ ] Is the full detailed plan (cartoon) completely traced onto the working surface?
- [ ] Has the climate/humidity been checked to ensure the plaster will not be damaged by dampness?
- [ ] Is the current plaster patch sized correctly for *one day* of work?
- [ ] Are all water-based pigments prepared prior to the plaster application?

### Composition & Style Checklist
- [ ] Do figures demonstrate individualized expressions and intentional gestures?
- [ ] Does the piece utilize perspective or foreshortening to interact dynamically with the viewer's space?
- [ ] Have you reviewed historical examples via `graphify_query` for specific stylistic references (e.g., Mannerist action, Baroque movement)?

### Restoration Checklist
- [ ] Are all retrieved fragments accounted for and mapped to the damage site?
- [ ] Have all modern additions or "improvements" been strictly avoided?
- [ ] Is the underlying structure (e.g., post-earthquake wall integrity) fully secured before surface restoration begins?
