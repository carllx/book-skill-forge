# Workflow: Reading Political Neutrality & Social Change in Portraiture

## Objective
To analyze how portraits reflect rapid socio-political turbulence, democratic shifts, and the artist's strategic neutrality during revolutionary periods (e.g., the American Revolution).

## Heuristics & Principles
1. **Sartorial Politics:** Clothing in portraits is highly politicized. The absence of imported luxury goods (like British lace) signifies ideological alignment (e.g., boycotts, democratic ideals).
2. **The End of Hierarchy:** Look for visual evidence of shifting social structures, where plainly dressed merchants are elevated to subjects of high art.
3. **Strategic Artist Neutrality:** Recognize that artists relying on commissions often adopt a neutral visual language to serve clients on opposing sides of a conflict.

## Steps for Analysis
1. **Contextualize the Turbulence:** Establish the political and economic friction of the era (e.g., taxes, boycotts, looming revolution).
2. **Analyze the Subject's Attire:** Examine the clothing for political signals. Is there a deliberate omission of luxury? Does it reflect democratic simplicity or patriotic boycotts?
3. **Assess the Artist's Client Base:** Look at the artist's portfolio. Are they painting figures from conflicting factions (e.g., patriots and loyalists)?
4. **Identify the Neutral Framing:** Determine how the artist frames the subject to avoid alienating either political side, focusing instead on character or profession rather than overt political iconography.
