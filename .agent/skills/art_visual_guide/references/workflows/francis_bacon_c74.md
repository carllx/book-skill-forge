# Workflow: Analyzing Francis Bacon & Dark Shadow Motifs

## Objective
To interpret thematic darkness, existential dread, and structural isolation in artworks using Francis Bacon's motif of the "Dark Shadow" as a heuristic lens.

## Heuristics & Principles
1. **The Existential Void:** Look for dark, unformed shadows placed conspicuously where traditional figures (like saints or mourners) would normally stand. This absence signifies an existential void.
2. **Subversion of Traditional Iconography:** Identify how classical or religious structures (like a crucifixion triptych) are subverted. If the structure expects salvation or holiness, how is it replaced by mortality or nihilism?
3. **Psychological Weight of the Shadow:** A shadow isn't just lack of light; it's a physical weight representing inevitable death without salvation.

## Steps for Analysis
1. **Identify the Core Structure:** Determine if the piece uses a classic framework (e.g., triptych, crucifixion, portrait). 
2. **Locate the Disruption:** Find the "dark shadow" or the grotesque element that replaces the traditional anchor of hope.
3. **Analyze the Void:** Ask *what is missing?* (e.g., saints, mourners, light). 
4. **Synthesize the Existential Message:** Conclude how the absence of expected elements and the presence of the shadow reinforce a theme of inescapable mortality.
