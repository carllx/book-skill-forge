# Workflow: Tracing the Elevation of a Medium (Ukiyo-e)

## Objective
To analyze how a mass-produced, commercial medium (like woodblock prints) is elevated to the status of high art through individual authorship and patron intervention, using Moronobu as a case study.

## Heuristics & Principles
1. **The Power of the Signature:** The act of signing a mass-produced work marks the shift from anonymous craftsman to recognized "Artist," fundamentally raising the medium's status.
2. **Commercial vs. Bespoke:** Understand the dual market: inexpensive, mass-produced base versions (black and white) versus premium, patron-commissioned versions (hand-colored).
3. **Founding a Genre:** Identify the "father" of a movement by looking for the individual who first consolidates popular themes (the "floating world") into a distinct, recognizable aesthetic.

## Steps for Analysis
1. **Examine the Economic Model:** Analyze the baseline format of the work (e.g., inexpensive, monochrome prints) and its intended mass audience.
2. **Identify Premium Interventions:** Look for hand-finished or customized details (e.g., hand-coloring) that indicate elite patronage and elevated value.
3. **Locate the Authorship Marker:** Find the artist's signature or seal, and evaluate how this branding shifts the work from craft to art.
4. **Define the Cultural Subject:** Connect the medium to the cultural phenomenon it captures (e.g., the hedonistic "floating world" of Edo Japan).
