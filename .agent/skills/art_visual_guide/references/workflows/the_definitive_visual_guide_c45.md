# Workflow: The Definitive Visual Guide & Indexing Mastery

## Prerequisites & Context

**Why and When:**
This workflow is designed for navigating comprehensive visual catalogs, managing extensive indices (such as artists and artifacts ranging from X to Z), and interpreting structural metadata from publication and editorial sections. Use this when analyzing historical visual artifacts, indexing vast collections, or contextualizing artifacts like the Celtic metalwork and Indian Yakshi figures. 

For deep dives into the foundational theories of visual arts indexing or historical art contexts, use the `graphify_query` MCP tool.

### 2. Navigating Historical & Cultural Transitions
Analyze objects as bridges between eras. 

> **Example: Celtic Transitions**
> Celtic art survived the disruptions of Germanic invasions, bridging the transition from paganism to Christianity. This is evident in their metalwork (weaponry, horse-trappings later adapted to chalices and shrines) and the evolution of their monumental high crosses and lavish manuscripts.

**Heuristics:**
- Look for continuous design patterns across different mediums (e.g., from weaponry to ecclesiastical objects).
- Identify pivotal artifacts that signify cultural synthesis.

### 3. Systematic Indexing (X, Y, Z)
When parsing exhaustive alphabetical indices (e.g., Zao Wou Ki, Yakshi Figure, Xia Gui):
- Differentiate between names of works and artists. Keep works in italics and artist names bracketed or clearly delineated.
- Use index segments to cluster related movements or regions across disparate pages.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

- **IF** an artifact’s visual meaning is ambiguous (e.g., a nature spirit vs. a warrior figure), **THEN** analyze secondary attributes (like weapons in a headdress) and cross-reference with contemporary artifacts (like Mohenjo-Daro seals) using `graphify_query`.
- **IF** encountering broken visual links or missing provenance data, **THEN** verify the editorial hierarchy (e.g., Art Directors, Picture Researchers) to locate the source library.
- **IF** historical continuity seems broken in a specific era (e.g., Celtic displacement), **THEN** trace the art style through alternative mediums like metalwork or stonework rather than geographic location.
