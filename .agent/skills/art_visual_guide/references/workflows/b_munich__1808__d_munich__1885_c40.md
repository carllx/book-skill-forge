# Workflow: Evaluating Biedermeier Genre Painting (Carl Spitzweg)

## Purpose
To analyze 19th-century anecdotal or genre paintings, focusing on how everyday life, humor, and social observation are rendered, using Carl Spitzweg as a model.

## Heuristics
- **Anecdotal Narrative**: The painting should tell a self-contained, often humorous or ironic story about everyday life.
- **Social Typology**: Identify specific social types or caricatures (e.g., the eccentric scholar, the romantic poet).
- **Intimate Scale and Focus**: Biedermeier art typically focuses on domestic, comfortable, or highly localized scenes rather than grand historical events.

## Execution Steps
1. **Narrative Extraction**: Identify the 'story' or punchline of the painting. What everyday situation is being depicted?
2. **Character Analysis**: Examine the subjects. Are they idealized, or do they possess quirky, individualistic, or gently mocked traits?
3. **Setting the Scene**: Analyze the environment. Look for meticulous details in interiors or quaint urban landscapes that frame the narrative.
4. **Cultural Context**: Connect the anecdotal elements to the broader bourgeois values of comfort, stability, and domesticity characteristic of the era.
