# 建造诗学与建构文化工作流 (Tectonic Culture Workflow)

## Prerequisites & Context

**WHEN to use this workflow:**
Apply this workflow when evaluating, designing, or analyzing architectural forms where the relationship between structure, construction tech, material expression, and poetic meaning is central. It shifts the focus from purely abstract spatial design to the "poetics of construction." 

**WHY use this workflow:**
To break free from superficial stylization or pure spatial abstractions and return to the fundamental ontological reality of architecture—where the act of building (tectonics) itself becomes an expressive, meaning-making endeavor. 

> [!NOTE]
> For deep-dive theoretical investigations into Gotfried Semper's four elements of architecture, Karl Bötticher's distinction between *Kernform* (core form) and *Kunstform* (art form), or detailed historiographies of 19th and 20th-century tectonic theories, use the `graphify_query` MCP tool. 

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Differentiate and Express Core Form vs. Art Form
*Heuristic: Do not let the structural necessity be entirely naked, nor let the ornamentation be arbitrary.*
- **Identify the Kernform (Core Form):** Understand the absolute structural necessity of the component (e.g., a wooden beam, a load-bearing column, the roof truss).
- **Develop the Kunstform (Art Form):** Articulate the representation of this structural logic through design. Ensure the visual expression (whether cladding, molding, or detailing) tells the story of the load-bearing process or the history of the building craft.
- *Example:* Instead of a plain beam, articulate the joints or use detailing that implies the tension/compression dynamics, much like the triglyphs in classical Doric orders evolved from wood construction.

### 2. Implement Semper's Four Elements Appropriately
*Heuristic: Structure your material palette based on the fundamental origins of habitation.*
- **The Earthwork (Stereotomics):** Use compressive, heavy materials (stone, masonry, rammed earth) for the base. This grounds the building and responds to topography. 
- **The Hearth:** Establish the social and thermal core of the building.
- **The Framework/Roof (Tectonics):** Utilize tensile, linear materials (wood, steel) to define the space.
- **The Enclosing Membrane:** Use lightweight, non-bearing materials (woven screens, textiles, glass, thin paneling) for spatial division. Emphasize the "woven" nature of the wall (*die Wand* vs *die Mauer*).

### 3. Anchor to Topography and Context
*Heuristic: The site is not an abstract plane; it is a lived, multi-sensory environment.*
- **Topographic Interpretation:** Instead of merely imitating nature (mimesis) or flattening the site, interpret its complexities. Use terracing, heavy bases, and winding paths to weave the architecture into the earth.
- **Sensory/Kinetic Engagement:** Design pathways that require bodily movement to comprehend the space. Vary ground textures (e.g., hard stone vs. mossy earth) to engage the pedestrian's physical sense of walking.

### 4. Engage Bodily Metaphor and Sensory Perception
*Heuristic: Architecture is not merely visual; it is tactile and acoustic.*
- **Tactility & Trace:** Select materials that register human touch and weather over time. Use textures that invite the hand. 
- **Acoustic Dimension:** Consider the reverberation of materials. Hard, enclosed stereotomic spaces sound different than lightweight tectonic enclosures. Use water features or specific material volumes to tune the "temperature" and "mood" of the space via sound.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

| Condition | Potential Issue | Solution / Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **If** the structural framework feels too detached or "engineered"... | Lack of *Kunstform* (Art Form) expression. The building is suffering from technical nihilism. | **Then** introduce tectonic articulation at the joints. Emphasize the connections (e.g., Scarpa's "worship of the joint") to narrate how the pieces come together. |
| **If** the building feels "rootless" or detached from its environment... | Failure to establish the Earthwork (Stereotomics). | **Then** re-evaluate the base. Anchor the structure with masonry, concrete, or stone that responds physically to the topography. |
| **If** the façade feels like a heavy, oppressive mass when it should be light... | Confusion between *Wand* (screen/woven wall) and *Mauer* (heavy fortress wall). | **Then** switch the material logic to a textile or screen-like cladding system that reveals its non-load-bearing nature. |
| **If** users pass through the space without noticing it... | Lack of sensory/kinetic friction. | **Then** alter the acoustic profile of the floor materials or create changes in elevation and texture to force conscious bodily engagement. |

## Verification Checklists

### Tectonic Integrity Check
- [ ] Are the load-bearing elements visually distinguishable from the space-enclosing elements?
- [ ] Do the joints and connections express the forces (tension/compression) acting upon them?
- [ ] Have you successfully avoided applying arbitrary ornamentation that contradicts the structural logic?

### Phenomenological & Topographical Check
- [ ] Does the foundation/base of the building establish a clear, physical dialogue with the terrain?
- [ ] Have you considered the acoustic "temperature" of the materials used?
- [ ] Do the surfaces invite and register physical touch and the passage of time (weathering)?
