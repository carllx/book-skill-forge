# Tectonic Culture and the Poetics of Construction Workflow

## Prerequisites & Context

**Why and When to Use This Workflow:**
This workflow is designed for architectural design, spatial analysis, and the phenomenological evaluation of built environments. It applies when shifting focus from purely abstract, "space-time" conceptions of architecture to the **tangible materiality, structural logic, and bodily experience** of construction—what Kenneth Frampton refers to as the "poetics of construction."

Use this framework when:
- Designing or evaluating the relationship between a building and its site (topography).
- Selecting materials and structural systems to convey specific expressive or symbolic meanings.
- Seeking to enhance the corporeal and tactile experience of a space (the *shintai* or sentient body).
- Balancing structural necessity (*Kernform*) with artistic representation (*Kunstform*).

## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Integrate Topos, Typos, and Tectonic
Architecture arises from the interplay of three vectors. Ensure your design actively addresses all three rather than relying on external discourses for legitimacy:
- **Topos (Site/Topography):** Do not treat the site as a blank slate or an abstract coordinate plane. Assess physical relations and interiorize the site's complexity.
- **Typos (Type):** Recognize the received cultural or typological form deposited by the lifeworld.
- **Tectonic (Construction):** Express the poetic potential of the constructional technique, rather than merely treating it as a neutral problem-solving mechanism.

### 2. Differentiate Tectonic and Stereotomic Modes
Leverage the fundamental distinction between the frame and the earthwork:
- **The Stereotomic (Earthwork):** Heavyweight, compressive mass (stone, brick, rammed earth, concrete). It marks the ground in perpetuity and gravitates toward the earth, often dissolving into it. Use it for foundations, massive walls, and anchoring the structure physically and psychologically.
- **The Tectonic (Frame):** Lightweight, tensile structure (wood, steel, woven materials, textiles). It encloses spatial matrices and aspires to the immateriality of the sky. 

> **Example:** In traditional construction (like the Mzab towns in Algeria or Semper's Caribbean hut), observe the stark contrast between the masonry foundation walls (stereotomic) and the lightweight palm/timber roof structures (tectonic).

### 3. Balance Kernform and Kunstform
Ensure a dialogue between the structural core and its artistic representation.
- **Kernform (Core Form):** The actual load-bearing structure or functional element (e.g., timber rafters).
- **Kunstform (Art Form):** The symbolic or representational expression of that structure (e.g., the petrified beam ends translated into the triglyphs of a classical stone entablature).

> **Actionable Heuristic:** Do not merely expose construction for the sake of technology (vulgar materialism). Articulate the building's corporeal presence to enhance the allusive poetics of its form. 

*Reference: Auguste Choisy's derivation of the Doric order from timber construction.*

### 4. Design for the Corporeal and Kinetic Experience
Architecture is experienced by the body in motion (*shintai*), not just the eye. Meaning is visceral before it is conceptual.
- **Tactility:** Incorporate hand-finish and varied textures. As Adrian Stokes notes, "people touch things according to their shape."
- **Kinesthetics:** Design the ground surface and circulation paths to be kinetically experienced through the gait (e.g., Dimitris Pikionis's Philopapou hillside park, which acts as a "stone tapestry").
- **Psycho-Acoustics:** Consider the reverberation and acoustic absorption of spaces, which dictate whether a volume feels warm or cold, intimately connected to the materials used.

*Reference: Dimitris Pikionis, detail of park paving, Philopapou Hill.*

> **Example:** Alvar Aalto's Säynätsalo Town Hall provides a masterful sequence of contrasting tactile experiences: starting from the stereotomic mass and dark, textured brick treads of the entry stair, the body shifts into the bright, timber-lined council chamber with its polished, flexing floors and the resonant smell of wood.

### 5. Ground Design in Ethnographic and Cosmological Roots
Acknowledge the deep cultural and cosmological associations of building materials and forms.
- Utilize Gottfried Semper's Four Elements conceptually: the earthwork, the hearth, the framework/roof, and the lightweight enclosing membrane.
- Use material transposition (*Stoffwechseltheorie*) deliberately to retain traditional symbolic value in new materials (e.g., woven motifs represented in masonry).
- Order space to reflect cosmological or cultural hierarchies, utilizing directional axes, spatial inversion, and material properties.

*Reference: Berber house orientation and internal/external inversion.*

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic

- **IF** the design feels sterile, "paper-like," or overly abstract, **THEN** re-evaluate the stereotomic grounding. Introduce heavier, compressive materials at the base or enhance the tactile qualities of the surfaces within immediate physical reach.
- **IF** the structural expression seems like pure, dry engineering without cultural resonance, **THEN** analyze the *Kunstform*. How can the structural necessity be articulated or ornamented to convey a mythic, empathetic, or constructional history?
- **IF** users are rushing through a space or failing to engage with it phenomenologically, **THEN** adjust the topography and kinetic cues. Use irregularly coursed pavers, subtle level changes, or acoustical dampening to force a more deliberate, bodily engagement.
- **IF** a new technological material is replacing a traditional one, **THEN** consider *Stoffwechseltheorie*. Ensure the new material either translates the tectonic/textile logic of the old to maintain symbolic continuity, or consciously breaks from it for a specific, identifiable poetic reason.

## Verification Checklists

### Site and Topography
- [ ] Has the building's footing explicitly responded to the specific nuances of the ground (the earthwork)?
- [ ] Is the transition from stereotomic base to tectonic frame clearly articulated in both structure and material?
- [ ] Does the site paving or hardscaping engage the body kinetically (via texture, sound, or physical resistance)?

### Materiality and Construction
- [ ] Are compressive (heavy, mass) and tensile (light, frame) elements used in a way that respects their inherent phenomenological properties?
- [ ] Is there a clear dialogue between the core functional structure (*Kernform*) and its artistic articulation (*Kunstform*)?
- [ ] Have you evaluated the non-visual (acoustic, olfactory, tactile) impacts of the materials chosen?

### Bodily Experience (Corporeal Metaphor)
- [ ] Does the spatial sequence offer contrasting bodily experiences (e.g., rough to smooth, dark to light, hard to yielding)?
- [ ] Are elements within hand's reach specifically designed for tactile engagement?
- [ ] Does the design accommodate the *shintai*—the sentient, active body experiencing the "here and now"—rather than just a disembodied conceptual eye?
