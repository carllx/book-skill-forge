# Architectural Tectonics & Constructional Poetics: The Perret-Mies Framework

## Prerequisites & Context
**Why and When to Use This Workflow**
Use this workflow when conceptualizing, designing, or evaluating civic and domestic structures that require a profound synthesis of structural rationality, material expression, and classical tectonic continuity. It is particularly crucial when navigating the tension between abstract avant-garde aesthetics and normative "art of building" (Baukunst). 

**Deep Theory Deferral**
This workflow translates the architectural philosophy of Auguste Perret and the early transitional phases of Mies van der Rohe into actionable design patterns. ## Comprehensive Guide & Best Practices

### 1. Hierarchical Articulation of the Skeleton
The structural framework must not be merely a hidden armature; it must serve as the primary ordering principle, echoing the rhythm and symmetry of an organism's skeleton.
*   **Differentiate by Civic Status:** Assign structural typologies based on the institution's public or private nature. Use a trabeated, expressed frame (or peristyle) for honorific and public buildings, and reserve load-bearing masonry for smaller, domestic works.
*   **Peristyle Placement:** Avoid universal, indiscriminate application of *pilotis*. Only deploy a peristyle to signal a monumental sequence or a building of high civic importance.
*   **Load-Bearing vs. Load-Borne Play:** Architecturally orchestrate elements to clearly depict what is supporting and what is being supported, taking inspiration from traditional timber framing (e.g., rafters resting on purlins, supported by beams).

> "The framework is to a building what a skeleton is to an animal... it must be composed, rhythmic, balanced and very symmetrical. It must be able to contain the most diverse and diversely situated organs and services demanded by function and appointment." — *Auguste Perret*

### 2. Modenature and Material Differentiation (In Situ vs. Precast)
When working with monolithic materials like concrete, exploit production techniques to differentiate structural systems visually and texturally.
*   **Surface Treatment as Tectonic Expression:** Use mechanical finishes (such as bush-hammering to expose stone aggregate) on the in situ structural frame. Contrast this rough, quarried texture with the smooth, precise finish of precast infill panels and window frames.
*   **Sequential Assembly Expression:** Let the visual depth and joint detailing of the facade arise logically from the assembly sequence (e.g., how a precast window frame is rebated into the in situ skeleton to provide weathering).
*   **Component Rationalization:** Design repeatable pieces as specific, customized tectonic units for the job, rather than adopting anonymous mass-produced components, thereby maintaining a high "art of building."

### 3. The Joint, the Capital, and Structural Honesty
Never hide the structural framework or simulate false structural members.
*   **Reverse Entasis:** To express a statical hinge at the base and rigid monolithism at the crown, taper columns inversely—making them narrower at the base and wider at the capital.
*   **Faceted Capitals for Monolithic Frames:** Do not default to plain butted joints. When transferring load from a square beam to a cylindrical shaft, design a geometrically precise transition (such as a faceted or imbricated capital) that translates the forces logically.
*   **Expose the Framework:** Treat the exposed skeleton as the building's primary ornament. Hiding it strips the architecture of its tectonic legitimacy.

### 4. Framing Human Space: The Vertical Window (*Porte-Fenêtre*)
Resist the automatic adoption of the horizontal panoramic window (*fenêtre en longueur*).
*   **Anthropomorphic Framing:** Use vertical French windows to frame the human figure, establish the decorum of the bourgeois interior, and induce the cadence of human movement.
*   **Spatial Exclusion and Focus:** Use the traditional window to provide a focused, perspectival connection to the outside world, preserving the psychological closure and intimacy of the interior.

> "Through the traditional window the interior discloses itself... the horizontal window 'condemns us to the view of an unending panorama,' whereas the vertical window stimulates us 'by letting us see a complete space: street, garden, sky. But above all, these openings may be closed.'"

### 5. Acoustic and Luminous Orchestration
Treat light and sound as tectonic building materials that define the character of a space.
*   **Translucent Vaulting:** In deep assembly spaces, simulate weightlessness by suspending a light armature (velarium) or using translucent screens to filter natural daylight from clerestory sources.
*   **Tactile Acoustics:** Use resonant materials like thin plywood paneling built out on timber battens to create an introspective, tactile acoustic environment—turning the room itself into an instrument.

### 6. Integration of Services into Tectonic Form
*   **Spatial Elision for Services:** Anticipate the need for modern services (HVAC, lighting) by designing structural voids. Use paired pilasters with voids between them for plenum ductwork, preventing the corruption of the primary structural aesthetic.

## If/Then Troubleshooting Logic
*   **IF** the structure feels overly monolithic and lacks human scale, **THEN** introduce a hierarchical secondary structure (e.g., precast window surrounds, timber battening) and differentiate the concrete surface texture (bush-hammering vs. smooth casting) to articulate the "building within the building."
*   **IF** applying an avant-garde aesthetic leads to a loss of tectonic legibility, **THEN** pull back to normative *Baukunst* (the art of building); reassert the expression of the structural joint, the load-bearing framework, and vertical fenestration.
*   **IF** a deep interior space requires uniform illumination without compromising the structural grid, **THEN** employ a translucent ceiling grid (velarium) suspended below clerestory lighting, echoing the orthogonal network of the basic skeleton.
*   **IF** addressing corner conditions in a heavily articulated peristylar system, **THEN** introduce redundant columns at the corners to visually terminate the structural rhythm, ensuring the facade reads as a complete classical order.

## Verification Checklists
- [ ] **Structural Primacy:** Does the framework serve as the fundamental ordering principle of the building?
- [ ] **Material Honesty:** Are structural materials exposed and articulated, rather than hidden behind applied decoration?
- [ ] **Tactile Modenature:** Is there a clear textural distinction between in situ load-bearing frames and precast/secondary infill panels?
- [ ] **Joint Translation:** Do capitals and joints logically resolve the transition between differing structural geometries (e.g., square beam to round column)?
- [ ] **Civic Decorum:** Does the choice of fenestration (vertical vs. horizontal) appropriately frame human action and establish the desired level of interior enclosure?
- [ ] **Service Integration:** Are modern services integrated gracefully into structural voids without violating the tectonic logic?
