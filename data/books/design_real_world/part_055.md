Design of Teaching and Training Devices for the Retarded, the Handicapped, and the Disabled: Cerebral palsy, poliomyelitis, myasthenia gravis, mongoloid cretinism, and many other crippling diseasesand accidents affect one-tenth of the American public and their families (20million people) and approximately 400 million people around the world. Yet the design of prosthetic devices, wheelchairs, and other invalid gear is by and large still on a Stone Age level. The cost of health care is rising astronomically. Regardless of who absorbs these costs in the long run, the fact remains that a great deal of the high expense can beattributed directly to bad design.

From time to time, illustrations of new biomedical equipment appear. Almost invariably these are "hi-style modern" cabinets, in nine delicious decorator colors, surrounding the same old machine. Hospital beds, maternity delivery tables, and an entire host of ancillary equipment are almost without exception needlessly expensive, badly designed, and cumbersome.
