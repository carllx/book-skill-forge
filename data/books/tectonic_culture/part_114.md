The long-drawn reading room of the Bibliotheque Sainte-Genevieve, though enclosed by heavy neo-Renaissance outerwalls, has a surprisingly fragile internal canopy with two longitudinallyparallel barrel vaults. The thin steelwork is asan addition ofanother era onto the heavy remains of the past. Though one still finds classica/ motifs on the co/- umns, thinas stems, these motifs areproportionally transformed into meresuperficial decorations. And when you come to think of it: the floral forms in the open-web semicircular trusses hardly cover up their structural character of diagonal bars; this is Art Nouveau avant la lettre!

The banded roofs goaround the corner instead of coming to a dead endat the end facade, and though they are relatedto barrel vaults, they don't divide the space into twoparallel strips;it stays one whole. The row of columns in the middle doesn't continue along the total length either, but keeps a distance from the ends without dividing the floor or interfering with the view through thereading room.

Labrouste, however, didn'tworkwithsemicircularbut quartercircular truss elements. He not only connects them to make semicirc/es butalso to make corners of 90 degrees,andwhat's more he connects them to theridgebeams, which he uses as complementary elements.

The influence of Viollet-le-Duc on late nineteenth-century practice is of course very extensive, as have tried to show in chapter 4, part Il, of my study Modem Architecture:A Critical History (London: Thames & Hudson, 1980). The entire Art Nouveau was indebted to Viollet-le-Duc in one form or another, particularly the French and Belgian adherents of this movement; above all, Hector Guimard, Victor Horta, Henri Sauvage,and Franz Jourdain. Clearly the Catalan modernismo school comprising Antoni Gaudi Cornet, Lluis Domenech Montaner, and Josep Puig Cadafalch also owes much to Viollet-le-Duc. Outside of this, the two masters of the proto-modern movement, Berlage and Auguste Perret, are also strongly indebted to the founder of structural rationalism.
