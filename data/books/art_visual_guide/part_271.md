# Ron Mueck

# b MELBOURNE, 1958

After working in children's television, special effects, and advertising for 20 years, Mueck started making sculpture in 1996, when he collaborated ona project with Paula Rego (see p.589). He gained international attention with hispoignant,half-ed sculpture Dead Dad, which was included in the Sensation exhibition at London's Royal Academy in1997.Itwas typical of Mueck's smaller works 一 striking for its concentrated hyper-naturalism.

Mueck has since made several over-sized figures, including the crouching Boy(1999) forthe Millennium Dome in London and Untitled(Big Man) (2000). Modelled in clayand cast in resins and silicone these figures achievea striking realism through Muerck's attention to surface detail.They are not alive, butwhen seen up close itis almost possible tosuspend disbelief.

Like many artists of his generation, Bill Woodrowused cleverly transformed junk in his early works. Saucepans, shopping trolleys, andbicycle frames were deconstructed Manufactured objects generated surprising hand-made companions, and suggested narrative connections. Woodrow's sculptures have grown more substantial overtime 一 and more frequently bronze-cast-but he has retained thisplayfulapproach, both to materialsand imagery. Poetic stories abound, especially in small and highly inventive crafted models. Thereare echoes of Surrealism,and his later works have perhaps become darker. Ifthere is a recurrent theme, it isthe resilient power of natureover intellect.
