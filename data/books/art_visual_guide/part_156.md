Today, Renoiris one of the best-loved artists of the 19th century,ut did not come easily tohim. His family were poorandattheage of13he began anapprenticeshipas aporcelain painter. After this he tooka job decorating blinds before enrolling at the studio of Paris-based Swiss painter Charles Gleyre (1806-74).There,hemet the core ofthe future lmpressionist group. Theylearned little from theirmaster'spolished, academicstyle, though Gleyre did encourage them to paintoutdoors Bythe late 1860s,

Self-portrait

Renoirwas doing this regularly, often painting by Monet's side. Fora time, theirsubjects and style were so similar thatit can be hard to distinguish between their pictures.

Renoir enjoyed some early success at the Salon (see p.336), but the financial rewards were meagre. Ironically, hepainted some ofhis most joyous scenes ata time when he was struggling to survive financially. Bythe late 1870s, however, his fortunes began to change, as he founda supportive dealerandanumber of loyal patrons. Gradually, Renoir abandoned his Impressionist roots adoptinga broader style thatplaced a greater emphasison drawing and structured composition.
