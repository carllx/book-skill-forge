# LIFEline

Haring was a New York-based artist who believed that art should be integrated in the life of the city.In 1980,he began to create drawings in subway stations, using white chalk on unused advertising panels covered with blackpaper. Haring produced hundreds of these rhythmical drawings, saying the subway was a laboratory" that helped himto evolve his spontaneous, free-flowing style. Healso drew on objects he found in the street 一 fridge doors, discarded cribs, or stuffed animals. In 1986, Haring opened the Pop Shop in SoHo,selling, ata low cost,Trs, toys,po magnets bearing his images. Haringwas diagnosed with AIDSin 1988and died in 1990,aged31.

# Jean-Michel Basquiat

# b NEW YORK, 1960; d NEW YORK, 1988

Borntoa Haitian father and Puerto Rican mother, Basquiat was brought up in New York. He had a troubled childhood 一 his parents separated when he was seven and he lived with his father, but often ran away. Asa child he learnt Spanish, French, and Englishand frequentlyvisited NewYork's museums.

Basquiat first gained notoriety for hiscryptic graffiti messages in SoHo in Manhattan. His rise to fame wasquick 1 Village Voice interviewed him about his graffiti in1978,he began painting on canvasaround 1980, andby 1983 hewas good friends withAndyWarholand included in the Whitney Museum's Biennial. Basquiat's painting consciously evoked the unfinished look of graffiti- and sometimes incorporated materials scavenged from the streets. His subject matter wasautobiographical with referencestttory legends, hip-hop and jazz music, and racism.

# Subjects

New Media artists have moved away from abstraction and towards an engagement with the world around them. Using photography,il,d video 一 technologies that record impressions of actual, physical reality 一 the lives of ordinary people canbe relayed in recorded images.

Common themes include people's interactions with their environment, relationships with one another, their hopes, fears, andself-image. People have also become not simplythe subjects, butactive participants in the creation of the works
